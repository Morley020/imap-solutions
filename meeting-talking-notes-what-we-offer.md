## 5. How we work

Use this five-step explanation when describing our delivery model:

1. **Understand the context** — We listen to the mandate, users, workflow, systems, data and constraints.
2. **Define the problem** — We clarify what needs to improve and what success should look like.
3. **Recommend a practical path** — We compare options, risks, priorities and responsibilities.
4. **Deliver or support implementation** — We can advise independently, lead a defined delivery, or work alongside the client’s existing team or suppliers.
5. **Document and hand over** — We clarify ownership, access, configuration, support, training and next steps.

Important positioning:

> We can provide an independent advisory engagement, a defined implementation, or a combination of both.

> We can work with existing systems and suppliers where access, responsibilities and support boundaries are clear.

> Every engagement is scoped around the client’s actual requirements. Hosting, licensing, privacy responsibilities, maintenance and response arrangements are agreed in writing.

## 7. Questions to ask the prospective client

Use these questions to move from a general introduction to a useful discovery conversation:

1. What organisational or programme problem are you trying to solve?
2. Who currently performs the work, and what does the workflow look like?
3. What systems, tools or suppliers are already in place?
4. Where are the biggest delays, risks, errors or information gaps?
5. What data is available, and who is responsible for it?
6. How many users, locations or field sites are involved?
7. What connectivity, power, device or access constraints should we consider?
8. What security, privacy, regulatory or institutional responsibilities apply?
9. Is the immediate need advisory, implementation, support—or a combination?
10. What would a successful first phase look like?

## 10. Language to use and avoid

### Prefer

- Practical and fit-for-purpose
- Secure, usable and documented
- Scoped engagement
- Existing systems and suppliers
- Human oversight and accountability
- Controlled pilot or product direction
- Agreed support arrangements
- Evidence for better decisions

### Avoid unless specifically verified in a signed engagement

- Guaranteed outcomes
- Zero downtime
- Universal under-one-hour response
- Fully autonomous AI
- Certified cybersecurity claims
- Completed pilot results for products still in development
- Claims that a product is government-approved or an official registry
